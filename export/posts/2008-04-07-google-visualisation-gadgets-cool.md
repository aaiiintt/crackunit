I discovered the world of visualisation gadgets through this excellent blog: [http://digitalurban.blogspot.com/](http://digitalurban.blogspot.com/)

They’re things that can basically turn a table full of boring data into interactive gadgets that you can publish and share as you like.

<iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/8EsFJu6p3P4" loading="lazy" allowfullscreen></iframe>

I thought it looked easy. So I tried it out.

I grabbed some European Internet Population [data from here](http://www.internetworldstats.com/stats9.htm). Copied and pasted it into Google Docs (via an Excel html import). Then added a column of [country codes from here](http://www.iso.org/iso/country_codes/iso_3166_code_lists/english_country_names_and_code_elements.htm).

Then just clicked insert gadget and bosh…

<iframe width="450" height="320" frameborder="0" scrolling="no" src="http://pg3j58c1-a.gmodules.com/ig/ifr?up__table_query_url=http%3A%2F%2Fspreadsheets.google.com%2Ftq%3Frange%3DB1%253AG28%26key%3DpGQrtj45SYWwhGE6DtJt8YA%26gid%3D0%26pub%3D1&amp;up_title=European%20Internet%20Population%20Data&amp;up_region=europe&amp;up__table_query_refresh_interval=0&amp;url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.google.com%2Fig%2Fmodules%2Fheatmap.xml" style="border: 1px solid #cccccc"></iframe>

It took about 15 minutes total to create something mildly useful. I’ve probably not picked the best information to display in this way – and the data is incomplete. But I was impressed with how simple it was.

There’s lots more types of gadgets – timelines, charts, and all sorts…

You can [read documentation of the heatmap gadget here](http://documents.google.com/support/spreadsheets/bin/answer.py?answer=91599).  
The [data source is here](http://spreadsheets.google.com/tq?range=B1:G28&key=pGQrtj45SYWwhGE6DtJt8YA&gid=0
).
